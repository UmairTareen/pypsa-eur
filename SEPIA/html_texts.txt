
# Texts for Scenario html file for Belgium

BE_cumulative_emissions_sce: <p>Although both scenarios follow the same trajectory, the gross residual emissions are significantly higher in the reference scenario, which imposes the use of negative emissions technologies such as DACCS or BECCS.</p><p>The comparison of cumulative emissions reveals significant differences across scenarios:</p><ul><li> In the negaWatt BE scenario, cumulative emissions from the industry sector are reduced by 35%, highlighting the effectiveness of resource efficiency and demand reduction strategies in lowering overall emissions.</li><li>In the negaWatt BE scenario, cumulative transport emissions are reduced by 50%, demonstrating the impact of efficiency measures and sustainable mobility strategies.</li><li> In the negaWatt BE scenario, cumulative emissions from the residential and tertiary sectors are higher. In contrast, the reference scenario requires heavy heat pump deployment from 2030 to stay within carbon budgets. Sufficiency measures and deep renovations allow continued use of fossil fuel technologies like gas boilers, with the power-to-heat ratio gradually increasing until 2050, offering a more realistic transition.</li><li>In the negaWatt BE scenario, a marked reduction is observed in the consumption of primary energy sources, including fossil fuels, biogas, and biomass, highlighting a shift towards more sustainable energy practices.

BE_comparison_sce: <p>The Negawatt-BE scenario highlighs different contributions to reach carbon neutrality by 2050: Energy efficiency allows to reduce the final energy consumption (Ref total); Energy suficiency further decreases the demand (Suff total); and the remaining consumption is provided by low-carbon energy carriers.</p>

BE_annual_costs_sce: <p>This bar chart represents the average total annual costs comparison between the optimised scenarios and 2019. These costs encompass the average total annualised investment costs and also operational expenses incurred annually during the transition period (2020 - 2050).</p><p>The total annual costs show a 33% reduction in the negaWatt BE scenario, the most notable differences in operational costs. The energy sufficiency scenario involves significant reductions in fossil fuels, in electricity imports and in hydrogen imports.</p><p>It is important to note that these are average values over the simulated periods, which explains why fossil fuels are still significant, although they are almost completely phased out by 2050.

BE_annual_clustered_costs_desc: <p>The total annual costs include investment (CAPEX) and operational (OPEX) expenses.</p> 

BE_investment_costs_sce: <p>Annual investment costs represent the average yearly investments needed for energy infrastructure and technologies from 2020 to 2050.</p><p>Comparing the scenarios reveals:</p><ul><li>The negaWatt BE scenario requires substantially lower investments in solar PV and onshore wind compared to the Reference scenario.</li><li>Offshore wind investment remains similar in both scenarios, as both reach maximum deployment.</li><li>Investments in power-to-heat, power-to-liquid, and power-to-gas technologies are all significantly reduced in the negaWatt BE scenario.</li><li>Compared to the 2019 situation, increase investment levels are required, even in the Negawatt-BE scenario.

BE_operational_costs_desc: <p>The annual operational costs include fuel costs and variable operational and maintenence costs for all technologies.</p><p>The comparison indicates that:</p><ul><li> The operational costs in 2019 are highest, while both negaWatt BE and reference scenarios reduce the operational costs due to a shift from fossil fuels to more electrification and hydrogen.</li><li>The NegaWatt BE scenario shows a reduction of 40% in annual operational costs compared to the reference scenario.

BE_cumu_investment_costs_sce: <p>The cumulative investment costs correspond to the total capital investment required for the whole transition period from 2020-2050. In the negaWatt BE scenario, cumulative investments for Belgium are 30% lower than in the reference scenario.</p>

BE_capacities_sce: <p>The installed capacities are compared for each energy source in Belgium at the end of the transition period (2050) with those in 2019:</p><ul><li>The negaWatt BE scenario results in lower solar PV and onshore wind capacities, reducing land and material use and supporting a more adaptable deployment pace.</li><li>Transmission lines remain essential for system flexibility and efficient energy distribution in both scenarios.</li><li>Nuclear capacity is phased out, and only limited CCGT capacity is retained by 2050 to provide flexibility using bio-fuels and e-fuels.

BE_storage_capacities_sce: <p>Storage capacities for different energy carriers in 2050 show a uptake from 2019 levels. The required battery and thermal energy storage (TES) however remains more limited in the Negawatt-BE scenario.</p><ul><li> District heating systems increasingly depend on seasonal storage, with Thermal Energy Storage (TES) playing a key role in balancing heat supply throughout the year.</li><li> TES is represented by large-scale water tanks and pit thermal energy storage, such as the facility in <a href="https://api.euroheat.org/uploads/PTES_in_Hoje_Taastrup_4ddf2bc6a3.pdf" target="_blank">Høje Taastrup, Denmark.</a></li><li> In both scenarios, TES capacity is substantial, reflecting its importance for the heating sector, especially if CCS is not permitted.</li><li>Gas storage remains at its current capacity of about 7.6 TWh, corresponding to the gas storage site of Loenhout. The possibility to retrofit this capacity into a hydrogen storage facility was evaluated but results in a less optimal overall solution.

BE_cc_capacities_sce: <p>This chart compares the required carbon capture and sequestration capacities for Belgium in 2050 under the Reference and negaWatt BE scenarios, relative to 2019.</p><ul><li> The Reference scenario requires large-scale deployment of carbon capture and sequestration (CCS), with total needs exceeding 25 MtCO₂eq/year—highlighting a strong dependence on technological solutions for emissions reduction.</li><li> In the negaWatt BE scenario, the need for carbon capture is much lower (below 5 MtCO₂eq/year), and there is no reliance on sequestration, reflecting a strategy focused more on demand reduction and systemic change.</a></li><li> The technology and market readiness level of Direct Air Capture (DAC) and other carbon capture technologies is still evolving, with many requiring further technological development and scaling before becoming commercially viable. Additionally, the geographic suitability for large-scale sequestration projects such as specific regions with ideal geological conditions for storing carbon plays a crucial role. Investments in both the technological infrastructure and geographic sites is necessary to ensure the successful implementation of these carbon capture and sequestration options, particularly in the Reference scenario where significant carbon capture and sequestration capacities are required.

BE_energy_ind_sce: <p>This chart illustrates Belgium’s energy independence in the Reference and negaWatt BE scenarios, compared to 2019.</p><p>Some key insights from the results include:</p><ul><li> In the negaWatt BE scenario, Belgium achieves an energy independence of 62%, compared to 51% in the reference scenario. In 2019, the country’s energy dependence was approximately 94%.</li><li> Energy imports in the negaWatt BE scenario are reduced by 58% relative to the reference scenario.</a></li><li> The composition of total energy imports shifts in the negaWatt BE scenario, with a move towards hydrogen infrastructure and greater electrification.
